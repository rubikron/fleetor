- **What changed.** `prompts/critic.md` gained three passages and one output bullet, and lost nothing: a paragraph drawing the in-remit / out-of-remit line inside `## Your remit`; a new `## The interview` section between the citation rules and the two traps; and an `INTERVIEW` bullet between `FINDINGS` and `UNCITED`. 119 lines / 7,222 bytes → 157 lines / 9,957 bytes. **No existing sentence was edited.** The six categories, the two traps, the archive citation rules, the anti-prescription paragraph and the `{archive}` placeholder are byte-identical. `prompts/orch.md`, `prompts/worker.md` and `crates/fleetor-core/src/brief.rs` (where `VERBS` lives) are byte-unchanged — `git diff --exit-code` on all three is empty — so `brief-cost` for this ticket is **0**, which is what WP-21 guardrail 4 asks for.
- **"Find mistakes and lapses of judgement" is not a reversal of the ban on judging, and the brief now says which side of the line a thing is on rather than leaving it to be inferred.** The arc doc calls this its most important sentence. A decision about **the run** — routing, decomposition, verification, what a pane concluded and on what basis — is in remit; a judgement about **the code** — whether it works, whether the design is right, whether the tests suffice — is out. Both worked examples in the brief are real findings from the Critic's first live run, and they are in the brief rather than only in the arc doc because the abstract rule is the half a model can satisfy while breaching: *"orch reported the run verified by receipts; no receipt existed"* is in, *"the retry logic should have been extracted"* is out. The paragraph is placed **between** the answer-key paragraph and the anti-prescription paragraph, touching neither, because the spike measured that removing the second produces "solid work, maybe more tests" — the exact failure this pane exists to avoid — and prescription is stage C's business.
- **Testimony is not record, and that is a rule about what the citation rule already forbids.** The brief's standing rule is *"you can cite what a pane wrote, not why it wrote it."* An interview answer is technically written by the pane, but afterwards, to someone assessing it, about its own conduct — so admitting it as archive evidence would quietly delete the rule while appearing to extend it. Three clauses hold the line: a third citation form `interview <pane> <ts>`, deliberately not in the shape of `events.json seq N` or a transcript line, so a reader can see at a glance which claims rest on the record; a finding may be **corroborated** by testimony and is **never established** by it, with the archive anchor kept on every finding; and **an answer that contradicts the archive is itself a finding** — of the archive-anchored kind, because the contradiction is visible in the record. That last clause is the one doing the work: it is where a lapse of judgement actually surfaces, and without it the interview can only confirm what the archive already showed. The brief tells the Critic to report the gap without deciding which side is true, which is the same refusal to grade the work stated one level up.
- **UNCITED survives the interview, and it records the answer.** A question asked and not settled goes there **with the answer recorded**, rather than the question alone or the answer alone. The arc's bar for stage A is a conversion rate out of that section — the first live report left six items there, three of them answerable by asking — so an UNCITED entry that drops what asking produced destroys the only measurement the stage has.
- **The brief teaches `fleet reply "<text>"` with no recipient, and that is D-077 showing up in prose rather than a style choice.** `fleet reply worker-3 "…"` is refused at parse; a brief that taught the recipient shape would teach a shape the CLI rejects, in a pane that reads its own stderr. The closed interview is described as failing at resolution with "no such pane" and named explicitly as *not* a finding — a Critic that filed the operator's own switch as a defect in the run would be reporting on the interface rather than on the fleet.
- **The veil was not weakened.** `prompts/critic.md` still contains none of the five needles `the_critics_brief_is_openly_named_and_names_no_evaluator_vocabulary` greps for, and the `answer key` exemption at line 14 is intact and still pinned by exact phrase. The word "judge" is used where "grade" would have read more naturally, because `grader` is one of the six needles and a sentence one letter away from tripping a tripwire is a sentence that will trip it during the next edit. No test, needle or exemption was touched.
