**Write the vision down.** An unwritten vision is not a vision, it is a mood — nobody can check it, delegate against it, or disagree with it. A fleet without one drifts, and nobody drifts anywhere they wanted to go.

**A vision is as much what it is not.** Pruning is what makes one real: every good idea kept is attention taken from the one that matters. Say what the fleet is deliberately *not* doing as clearly as what it is.

**The vision is the filter.** Distractions arrive dressed as opportunities, and every yes is a no to something else. Run each choice — what to delegate, what to defer, what to refuse — through the written vision rather than through whatever is nearest.

**One vision, held by everyone.** More than one vision is division: four workers each pursuing their own reading of the goal is slower than one pursuing the right one. Give them the same vision in the same words.

**Resistance is information, not a verdict.** A failing test, a design that fights back, a worker who disagrees — none of those are reasons to abandon the vision. They are usually the shape of the real problem becoming visible for the first time.

**ME → WE.** A vision that ends with you was never big enough. Ask what would leave the fleet better, not only what would finish this turn.
