- **What changed.** A ninth `fleet` verb: `fleet handoff --built "<what the fleet built>" --evidence "<how anyone could check it>" [--evidence …] [--open "<what is unfinished>"]`, by which `orch` declares the whole confirmed goal met. It appends one `FleetEvent::Handoff` and does nothing else — new `Op::Handoff`, new `crates/fleetor-core/src/handoff.rs`, a sixth event variant with its `ui/src/fleet/types.ts` mirror and a coral, railed Activity row. `VERBS` goes 8 → 9; the clap enum, the wire, both prompt files, the pinned-literal brief tests and `src-tauri/src/prompts.rs`'s override fixture all moved in the one commit the contention rule requires.
- **Why it is not called `done`, and why the default lost.** The obvious name was taken: `fleet done <task-id> "<check>"` is a *worker* closing one block with a receipt (D-050). Naming the mission-level act with a near-synonym of the block-level one is precisely the drift the `accepted`/`recorded`/`delivered` discipline exists to prevent — one name per concept, and `done` already has its concept. `handoff` also carries the honest framing: the fleet's work is over and the operator takes it back. The two verbs are the two a model could most plausibly confuse, so each brief names the one that is **not** its pane's (`both_briefs_separate_closing_a_block_from_handing_the_work_back`), and the CLI refuses a worker's `fleet handoff` locally with a sentence naming `fleet done` — the same contract-on-the-sender check, in the same place, as `done.rs`'s refusal of `orch`.
- **`recorded`, deliberately, and not a fourth `OpResult`.** There is no pty to reach, and D-051 settled that stretching `accepted` over a record-only op dilutes the one word Tier 1.5 keeps; `delivered` is never rendered by anything. So `recorded` — reusing `OpResult::Recorded { record_id }`, because D-051's whole argument is that a second variant meaning "entered the log, no pty exists" would be two spellings of one fact. Three callers now share it: a task claim, a message to `operator`, and a handoff.
- **A sixth `FleetEvent` variant rather than a message to `operator`.** `task.rs`'s tripwire list is the bar, and it is cleared the same way `Task` clears it: **nothing reads a handoff back to permit, order or refuse anything.** `crates/fleetor-server/tests/handoff.rs` counts that instead of asserting it — a handoff sends the app zero `AppCommand`s, and a `fleet send` is byte-identical at the pty and in the log before and after one. `Hub::handoff` is not `async` and never touches `self.app`, the signature-level tell `Hub::task` and `Hub::record` already carry. It is not a `Message` to the operator because a message is something a pane *said* to an addressee; a declaration rendered in the message record would put words in `orch`'s mouth, and prose would flatten evidence that is worth keeping as a list. The variant has **no `accepted` field** for the same reason `recorded` is the word.
- **The worker brief was touched, and that was forced rather than chosen.** `brief::require_verbs` validates the rendered *worker* brief against the same `VERBS` list, so a verb `worker.md` never names makes the shipped template fail its own validation. Three options: one line in `worker.md`, per-pane `VERBS` lists, or leaving `handoff` out of `VERBS` and breaking the clap pin. The one line won — it is useful prose rather than a formality (it is the altitude split, said where a worker will look), while the other two trade one stable CLI contract for a shape in which a verb can end up pinned by nothing.
- **The veil held, and the test that keeps it grew.** Nothing `orch` is told mentions an evaluator, an assessment, a retro or a rubric; the prose says the verb reports to the operator, which is the whole truth from the fleet's side. WP-16's `no_pane_is_briefed_about_dev_mode` covered the *mode*; `no_pane_is_briefed_about_the_evaluator` now covers the thing behind it, over the same seven files that become words a pane is told. `review` is deliberately not on that word list — peer review is real, taught and worker-facing (WP-06).
- **Cost, measured — the sixth verb-adding overrun, and the ledger held again.** `count.py` (DeepSeek Flash tokenizer): orch **3,366 → 3,668 (+302)**, worker **2,099 → 2,139 (+40)**; combined +342 against a pre-prose guess of ~80, i.e. **~4.3×** — inside D-053's 3.8–4.6× band for a package that adds a verb, and for the reason that ledger gives: a verb costs what its *rules* cost. The syntax is one line; the rules are the altitude split, the mandatory evidence, the check-before-you-claim instruction and the consequence attached to each.
- **The cap is restated, not silently edited: orch 3,500 → 3,800; worker unchanged at 2,200.** D-053's reversal clause requires a re-measurement and an explicit statement, and this is it. 3,800 keeps the same discipline D-056 used — thin headroom over measured reality (+132, ~3.6%) so accidental growth still trips it, rather than a round number chosen to stop the question being asked. The measured worker figure (2,139) sits 61 under its unchanged cap. Stated for context and not as licence: the operator's position is that the cap is a drift tripwire rather than a ceiling and that ~10k would be acceptable, so the prose was written to say what the verb needs and then measured — not contorted to fit.
- **What would reverse it.** A live run in which `orch` hands back on a full board rather than a met vision — the fix is the prose ("the finish line is the vision the operator confirmed, not the last block on the board" is already the clause), not the verb. Or WP-15 finding it needs something the event does not carry, which is a field on `Handoff`, not a second verb. If the two verbs are observed being confused in practice despite both briefs naming the other, the reversal is a rename of one of them — cheap, because `VERBS`, the clap enum, the wire tag and the pinned tests all move together by construction.
- **Verified:** 210 workspace tests green (49 CLI, 108 core, 26 pane-messaging, 7 task-board, 3 handoff, 4 event-bus, 12 db, 1 ipc) and 121 shell tests green (107 lib, 3 dev-mode, 11 real-pty), `npx tsc --noEmit` and `npx vite build` clean, `count.py` re-run before and after. **Not verified:** nothing has run this verb inside a live fleet — the Activity row was written against building.md §7's rules and never seen on screen, which is the same gap D-061 records for the dev-mode band (this repo has no frontend test framework).
