id: D-091
title: "the orchestrator seat spawns `--permission-mode auto`"
phase: "meta"
status: alive
---

- **What.** Operator's call, 2026-09-11, choosing option (a) of three: `orch_command_with` builds its seat with `.with_permission_mode("auto")`, so the operator's own seat comes up in auto mode rather than asking. Supersedes the one line of D-030/D-052's asymmetry that said otherwise; every other line of it — the operator's real `HOME`, no Fence, no worker PATH, no `ANTHROPIC_*` override, their account and their model — stands untouched.
- **Why the old default lost.** Its stated reason (`spawn.rs`, and WP-15's re-statement) was that `orch` is watched by an operator who approves its calls. That is still true and stopped being a reason: being watched is why auto-approve is *acceptable* here, not why it should be withheld. The friction it bought was the operator hand-approving their own orchestrator's every call.
- **This is a genuine widening of Tier 1.7, said plainly so it is not later discovered.** Every previous auto-approve in this codebase was argued as a *narrowing* — workers are fenced into a worktree, the evaluator and the critic get their own working directory alone. `orch` is the opposite case: the operator's real `HOME`, no Fence, and the widest guardrail roots in the app (`placement/mod.rs`'s `guardrail_notices` gives it `roots_for(cwd, shell, fence_allow)`). What now stands between an auto-approved `orch` and the target repo is the write guardrail and nothing else. `building.md` §9.2 names widening auto-approve as an escalation trigger; this entry is that escalation, taken deliberately.
- **No vendor branch was needed, and that is the seam working.** `Seat::permission_mode` is read by the generic `command_args`/`resume_args` bodies against `Posture::permission_flag`; codex's is `None` (`placement/codex.rs:536`), so a codex orchestrator takes nothing from this line. A reopened orch keeps the posture for free, because D-089 already put `resume_args` under the containment property.
- **Asserted, not merely allowed.** `orch_takes_a_config_dir_and_none_of_the_worker_isolation` had the inverse assertion; it now requires the flag, so a future change that drops it fails rather than passes quietly.
