  The sum of the five `brief-cost` guesses (510 combined) against the sum of what those five packages actually spent (2,018 combined) reconciles to **~4.0× overall** — one number, not five separate overruns. WP-04, the one package that added no prose worth calling protocol (a counter Notice line), is the sole outlier under budget. The other four all added a verb or a protocol (cmd, task, done+review+merge, operator), and every one of those landed between 3.8× and 4.6×, confirming Q-2/Q-3's reading: **a `brief-cost` guess written before the prose is fiction; a verb costs what its rules cost, not what its syntax costs.**
- **Contextualized, not just counted.** Post-prune: orch **2,645** tokens, worker **2,099** tokens.
  - Against `WORKER_WINDOW_TOKENS = 128_000` (D-046, the number a worker's own context gauge is measured against): the worker's whole system prompt is **1.64%** of its context window before a single message arrives. Even the larger orch figure, held to the same yardstick for scale, is **2.07%**. Neither figure is close to a window that would visibly crowd out real work.
  - Against the **1,485-token** block of Claude Code's own guidance that `--system-prompt` stopped sending (D-043, the WP-02 baseline point): orch is **1,160 tokens past parity** (1.78×) and worker is **614 tokens past parity** (1.41×). This is the number Q-3 flagged as "orch ~1,050 past parity" back at WP-06 and asked whether it was a problem.
  - **Verdict, stated in measurable terms rather than vibes:** nobody has measured a behavioral cost from either figure — no worker has been observed truncating, ignoring, or mishandling any brief content because of its size, and the live shakedown (this package's other half, operator-gated) is the first place such a cost could show up. What *is* measurable is that the prompt occupies under 2.1% of the budget it has to share with an entire task, and the "past CC parity" comparison is a reference point, not a demonstrated regression — CC's own 1,485-token guidance was calibrated for a generic coding agent, not a fleet member with a verb list, a task board and a review protocol to learn. On the numbers alone: **not a problem today; worth capping so it cannot become one silently.**
- **The Tier 2 cap.** `PROMPT_BUDGET_ORCH_TOKENS = 2,800`, `PROMPT_BUDGET_WORKER_TOKENS = 2,200` (DeepSeek Flash tokenizer, measured by `count.py`; not yet wired into a runtime check — see the menu doc for that as a candidate). Headroom above today's measured, post-prune reality: **+155 orch (~5.9%)**, **+101 worker (~4.8%)**. Deliberately thin, not aspirational: Q-2 and Q-3 already established there is no meaningful fat left (WP-06's own pre-measurement tightening pass recovered only 6% of *its* addition; this package's conservative pass recovered 0.5% of the *entire* prompt — see below), and no package left on the roadmap adds a verb before the live shakedown, so the likeliest next change to these files is a shakedown finding, not a feature. A cap with real headroom the next edit immediately breaks teaches nothing; a cap this close to reality catches accidental growth (a rewritten paragraph that quietly grew, a fragment edited without checking the render) while still leaving room for one small, deliberate addition without an immediate rewrite fire drill.
  - **What would reverse it.** Either direction: (1) the operator picks one of `prompt-budget-menu.md`'s cut candidates, the prompt shrinks, and the cap should come down to track it — a cap that stays high after a real cut is measuring nothing; or (2) a genuine new protocol (a verb, a new fragment) needs to land after the shakedown, in which case its own `decisions.md` entry re-measures and states the new cap explicitly, the same discipline WP-03 through WP-07 already used. A cap is not raised by editing this line without a re-measurement — that is exactly the estimate-before-prose pattern this whole ledger exists to stop.
- **The prune — conservative, and it shows.** Wording-level tightening only: redundant modifiers cut (`"their own context and their own view"` → `"...and view"`), a few `"rather than X"` clauses shortened to `"not X"` where the clause was not a pinned literal, one filler clause dropped (`"and get on with it;"`), one paraphrase shortened without changing what it says (`"say how what you built serves the part of the vision the block names"` → `"say how it serves the block's part of the vision"`). Nothing removed: every verb, every rule, the anti-amplification clause, the full delivery contract (`accepted` / `recorded` / `not a promise`), and the authority bound (`**their word is final**`) are untouched, byte-identical to before this package. Result: orch 2,668 → **2,645** (−23, −0.86%), worker 2,102 → **2,099** (−3, −0.14%); combined −26 tokens, **−0.5% of the whole prompt budget**. This is the fourth tightening pass in the ledger (after D-045's −6/−27, D-047's pass folded into its number, D-050's −21/−25) and by far the smallest — the expected shape once a prose that has already been tightened three times is tightened a fourth. It is further confirmation, not a new finding: **there is no fat left; what remains is protocol, and cutting it is the operator's menu, not a builder's edit.**
- **What the sum answers from WP-09's own performance criteria.** "Every verb still validated" — `validate_orch`/`validate_worker` both still pass on `DEFAULT_ORCH`/`DEFAULT_WORKER` (`the_baked_in_templates_validate`), and every one of the ~45 pinned-literal tests in `crates/fleetor-core/src/brief.rs` is unchanged and green — none were loosened, shortened, or removed to make room. "Both prompts ≤ cap after pruning" — 2,645 ≤ 2,800 and 2,099 ≤ 2,200, both true with the stated headroom.
- **Verified:** 186 workspace tests, 92 shell tests green (unchanged counts — this package edited only `prompts/orch.md` and `prompts/worker.md`, no Rust source). `tsc --noEmit` and `vite build` clean. `examples/system-prompt-spike/count.py` re-run before and after the prune, numbers above are its output.
