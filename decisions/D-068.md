- **What changed.** `prompts/orch.md` §"What arrives" gained a paragraph: a reply written as ordinary output reaches nobody, `fleet reply` is what gets back to the sender, and that holds for a sender whose name orch does not recognize — if a message arrived, the sender is a real pane, and `fleet reply` finds it whether or not `fleet roster` lists it.
- **The gap it closes.** orch's brief enumerated only workers and `operator`, so a message from any other pane got answered into orch's own transcript and reached nobody. Observed live: the evaluator's two interview questions (seq 15, 16) had no reply in the log. `worker.md:23` already carried the equivalent ("This is your usual move"); orch's did not.
- **Why it is worded without naming the sender.** `tests/dev_mode.rs::no_pane_is_briefed_about_the_evaluator` scans this file and fails on the word, and the veil (WP-12) requires it. The clause is general on purpose — it is true of any non-enumerated pane, which is the property `pty.rs::roster` documents ("not enumerated, and not unreachable").
- **What would reverse it.** Evidence that orch replies to unknown senders without being told, or a decision that non-members should not be able to reach orch at all — which would be a delivery-path refusal and collides with Tier 1.4.
- **Brief cost, measured not guessed (D-053).** `examples/system-prompt-spike/count.py`: orch **3,668 → 3,749**, worker unchanged at 2,139. The guess before writing the prose was ~65; actual 81, ~1.25× — well inside the band, and consistent with D-053's finding that a package adding *no verb and no protocol* lands near its estimate while verb-adding ones land 3.8–4.6× over. **This leaves 51 tokens under the 3,800 cap**; the next orch-brief change measures first or restates the cap.
